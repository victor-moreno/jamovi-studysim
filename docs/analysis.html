Analysing the data in jamovi

Each design calls for its own analysis, and not all of them are in base jamovi. The table says which analysis fits each design and where to find it: in base jamovi, in another module of the jamovi library (Modules ▸ jamovi library), or in one of the author’s modules (jamovi-modules-vm). Below it, one worked exercise per design: the scenario, what to do in jamovi, and what the answer key says a correct analysis should find. The Epidemiology guide explains each design, its strengths and limitations, and how to read its parameters; Causal diagrams explains how to choose what to adjust for.

Design, analysis and module

design layout of the data analysis in jamovi
Cross-sectional one row per person logistic (binary), linear (continuous), Poisson (count) regression Regression ▸ Logistic Regression ▸ 2 Outcomes, Linear Regression; Poisson: GAMLj Generalized Linear Models
Cohort (event) time, status 0/1 (2 = competing event) Kaplan-Meier, Cox regression prognosis Prognosis ▸ Kaplan-Meier, Cox Regression
Case-control case/control logistic regression; odds ratio of a 2×2 table Regression ▸ Logistic Regression ▸ 2 Outcomes; Frequencies ▸ Independent Samples (odds ratio)
Matched case-control set = matched set conditional logistic regression condLogReg Regression ▸ Logistic Regression ▸ Conditional (Matched), matching variable set
Nested case-control set = risk set conditional logistic regression (it estimates the hazard ratio) condLogReg, matching variable set
Case-cohort subcohort yes/no, time, status Cox regression with Prentice weights and robust variance not in a jamovi module: the Rj editor with survival::cch()
Prognosis time from entry, status Kaplan-Meier, Cox; with a competing event, cause-specific Cox (status 2 censored) prognosis; cumulative incidence of competing risks: Rj with cmprsk
Diagnostic accuracy status, markers ROC curve, AUC, sensitivity and specificity at a cut-off meddecide (ClinicoPath) or psychoPDA Test ROC; at a cut-off: Compute a positive/negative column, then Frequencies ▸ Independent Samples
Method comparison y_a, y_b Bland-Altman limits of agreement blandr or SimplyAgree; or Compute the difference and the mean, then Descriptives and a scatterplot
Randomised trial arm, outcome risk difference, relative risk, NNT (binary); t-test or ANCOVA (continuous); Kaplan-Meier and Cox (event) Frequencies ▸ Independent Samples; T-Tests, ANOVA ▸ ANCOVA; prognosis; interim looks: jmsequential
Cluster randomised trial cluster mixed (multilevel) model with a random intercept per cluster GAMLj Mixed Models (continuous), Generalized Mixed Models (binary, count)
2×2 factorial trial two arms ANOVA or regression with both treatments and their interaction ANOVA ▸ ANOVA; Regression ▸ Logistic Regression
2×2 crossover long: one row per period; wide: one per person paired t-test (wide); mixed model with treatment and period (long) T-Tests ▸ Paired Samples; GAMLj Mixed Models
Longitudinal trial long: one row per visit; wide: one per person linear mixed model with all visits (long); repeated-measures ANOVA on completers (wide) GAMLj Mixed Models; ANOVA ▸ Repeated Measures ANOVA
Before-after y_before, y paired t-test of the change T-Tests ▸ Paired Samples
Ecological one row per region regression of the regional rate on regional means and percentages Compute the rate (cases / people), then Regression ▸ Linear Regression

Repeated measures (Design options) gives the crossover and longitudinal trials one row per person (wide), which jamovi’s paired t-test and repeated-measures ANOVA need; the default long layout is the one for mixed models.

Export codebook saves the data dictionary next to the CSV, since a CSV keeps no column descriptions.

Worked exercises

Each exercise uses the default seed (1) and the scenario’s default options. The tables are the answer key’s what this design should estimate: a correct analysis of a study of the size given should land near Adjusted, within its own confidence interval.

Cross-sectional: diabetes in Pima women

Diabetes in Pima women, cross-sectional, 700 women, all variables. Fit Regression ▸ Logistic Regression ▸ 2 Outcomes with diabetes as the outcome and the measured variables as covariates (Odds ratio under Model Coefficients). Then fit one variable at a time and compare each crude odds ratio with its adjusted one.

Cohort: coronary heart disease

Coronary heart disease, cohort of 3,000. In prognosis, Kaplan-Meier of time and chd by smoking, then Cox Regression with the measured variables. Leave out hdl (untick it under Variables) to see the hazard ratio for sex change: HDL is a mediator of sex, so the estimate moves towards the total effect, which is what the Adjusted (DAG) column estimates.

Nested case-control and case-cohort: Wilms tumour relapse

Relapse of Wilms tumour, nested case-control with 3 controls per case: condLogReg with set (the risk set) as matching variable gives hazard ratios, with no rare-disease assumption. The case-cohort version needs a weighted Cox model: in Rj, survival::cch(Surv(time, relapse) ~ histology + stage, data = data, subcoh = ~ subcohort == "yes", id = ~ id, cohort.size = …, method = "LinYing") (the cohort size is in the Study summary).

Prognosis with a competing event

Breast cancer prognosis, outcome Death from breast cancer, prognosis design. In prognosis, Cox Regression of time and the status: the status has a 2 for a death from other causes; treat it as censored to estimate the cause-specific hazard ratios of the key. 1 − Kaplan-Meier then overstates the cumulative incidence of the event.

Diagnostic accuracy: PSA

PSA and prostate cancer, diagnostic, 500 men. In meddecide or psychoPDA, the ROC curve of psa against prostate_cancer. The key gives the AUC and, at a cut-off, the sensitivity, specificity and predictive values; Compute psa > 5.63 and cross it with the disease (Frequencies ▸ Independent Samples) to check them by hand. Then set Test-negatives verified (%) to 20 (Biases): among the verified, sensitivity is far too high.

Method comparison: two blood pressure devices

Blood pressure in US adults, method comparison of sbp. In blandr or SimplyAgree, the Bland-Altman analysis of sbp_a and sbp_b: the mean difference, the limits of agreement and the proportional bias (B grows faster than A).

Randomised trial: indomethacin and pancreatitis

Rectal indomethacin to prevent post-ERCP pancreatitis, randomised trial of 600. Frequencies ▸ Independent Samples with treatment and pancreatitis (Comparative measures: relative risk, difference in proportions); the number needed to treat is 1 / risk difference. The key’s trial table gives the true risks: note that the marginal odds ratio (what the 2×2 table estimates) is closer to 1 than the conditional one the data were generated with, with no confounding at all.

Cluster randomised trial: handwashing in Karachi

Handwashing and childhood pneumonia, cluster trial. In GAMLj Generalized Mixed Models, a Poisson model of pneumonia with soap as a fixed factor and cluster as the cluster variable. Ignoring the clusters (a plain Poisson regression) gives confidence intervals that are too narrow.

2×2 factorial: aspirin and beta-carotene

Aspirin and beta-carotene in male physicians, factorial. prognosis Cox Regression with both treatments; adding their interaction tests whether one changes the other’s effect.

Crossover: an antihypertensive

Antihypertensive drug, crossover of 30 people with Repeated measures: wide. T-Tests ▸ Paired Samples of sbp_placebo and sbp_amlodipine. With the long layout, GAMLj Mixed Models of sbp with drug and period as fixed factors and id as cluster gives the same estimate and the period effect.

Longitudinal trial: succimer for lead exposure

Succimer for lead-exposed children, longitudinal. Long layout: GAMLj Mixed Models of blood_lead with treatment, visit (as a factor) and their interaction, id as cluster. The key’s Crude is the naive comparison of the completers at the last visit, Adjusted the mixed model with every visit.

Before-after: metformin

Metformin vs placebo on HbA1c, before-after, enrolling only people above the 50th percentile. T-Tests ▸ Paired Samples of hba1c_before and hba1c: the mean change is larger than the true effect, because of regression to the mean.

Ecological: water hardness and mortality

Water hardness and mortality, ecological. Compute the death rate per 100,000 (death / population * 100000) and regress it on the region means and percentages (Regression ▸ Linear Regression); compare the regional slope with the true individual effect in the key: the gap is the ecological bias.

Simulating many studies

Compare designs with Replicate studies per design draws hundreds of studies of each design and reports, for each estimate, its mean, bias, spread (the standard error a study does not see), power and the coverage of its confidence intervals: chance and bias side by side.
